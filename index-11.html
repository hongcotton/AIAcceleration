The Problem

Everyone's using AI.
Hardly anyone has a plan.

Most of your people have tried ChatGPT. A few are running experiments. But the leadership team hasn't agreed what AI is for, where it should go first, or what's stopping it. This sprint gets that agreement.

Dabbling isn't a strategy

Silo'd experiments and personal subscriptions don't add up to anything. Somebody has to decide what AI is actually for in this business.

Hype and fear in the same room

Some of your team are bullish, some are anxious, and most aren't saying which. You can't plan around a mood you can't see.

Too many ideas, no priorities

Every function has a use case. Without a way to rank them on impact and effort, nothing gets backed properly.

The barriers go unnamed

Budget, data, ownership, KPIs. The things that kill AI projects rarely get said out loud until a pilot quietly dies.

How it works

The gap is the answer.

All the way through the sprint, people place dots and straws to show what they think. Then we compare. Where you are against where the company is. What business wants from AI against what yours does. How the room felt at the start against how it feels at the end.

The team gets a structured conversation about AI. The leader gets a map of where everyone actually stands.

Every gap on the board is a conversation the leadership team needs to have. The sprint makes sure they have it.
The Sprint

Nineteen exercises across two sessions.

Session 1 — Where We Stand Half day
01

Before we start

Glass full, half-full, nearly empty, fallen over, or pigs might fly? Everyone drags a straw to show how they feel about AI right now.

Business context
02

Audit

Where are you today?

An adoption scale runs from “compliance says no” to “Big Tech, all in”. Each person places one dot for where the company is and one for where they are. Then: which AI tools have you actually used, and what have you learned?

The team does

Plots the company and themselves on the scale.

The leader sees

The gap between the two dots. People ahead of the company are your champions. People behind it need bringing along.

03

Headwinds & Tailwinds

What's pushing the business forward and what's holding it back, inside and out. Before anyone talks about AI, the team agrees what the business is actually up against.

04

The World You Do Business In

Customers, competitors, technology, offering, economics, culture. What's changing slowly, what's changing fast, and where AI fits as a disruptor.

AI context
05

What is AI?

One word, a very big concept. The team writes what AI means to them, then votes, so everyone is working from the same definition.

06

What's AI to you?

AI from a personal point of view, before it gets organisational. How it has already turned up in people's lives.

07

What's AI to business?

Why is every business focused on AI?

Sixteen motivations, from saving money and staying ahead to cutting staff and “don't know yet”. Everyone votes on why businesses in general want AI, then votes again for this company.

The team does

Votes twice: once for business in general, once for us.

The leader sees

Where the company's reasons differ from the market's. That difference is your AI positioning.

08

Framing the AI Ambition

Each person writes their own “How might we use AI to…” statement. Think big. Then the team votes on the ambition worth chasing.

09

Are We Ready?

Do you have the building blocks in place?

Six attributes on two triangles: budget, appetite and corporate culture; people and skills, need, and leadership. Everyone scores each from 0 to 100.

The team does

Scores readiness, one dot at a time.

The leader sees

A shape. Where the triangle collapses is where the AI plan will stall unless you fix it first.

Session 2 — What We'll Do Half day
Risks & opportunities
10

The AI Opportunity Universe

Seven lenses: customer, sales and marketing, operations, people, financial, reputation, long-term strategy. What could AI deliver in each?

11

The AI Risk Universe

The same seven lenses, flipped. What happens if we don't act? This is about seeing the threats before they arrive.

12

Use Case Opportunities

Twenty-two proven AI use cases across customer insight, internal processes and operations. The team turns them into specific ideas, framed as current state and future state.

13

Your Value Chain

From procurement to customer service, stage by stage. Where are the bottlenecks today, and what would the ideal look like with AI?

14

Team Breakout: Prioritise

Impact against ease

Teams take the top ten ideas, plot them on an impact and ease-of-implementation matrix, then commit to the two the organisation should do first, with a rationale for each.

The team does

Argues it out and picks two.

The leader sees

Which ideas survive scrutiny across every team, and which only one person believed in.

Making it real
15

Barriers to Adoption

Buy-in from the boss, no AI champion, the data isn't ready, it's not in anyone's KPIs. The team votes on the barriers most likely to bite, then works out how to get round the top four.

16

Capabilities

What will it take? Every capability the plan needs is sorted into existing, build, acquire, or partner.

17

How Might We Change?

Priorities, skills, technology, training, talent, investment, leadership support. Where the organisation has to move to make the plan happen.

18

Making a Plan

What are we actually going to do in the next three quarters? Two or three moves each for Q1, Q2 and Q3.

19

Before You Go

The same question, at the end

Glass full, half-full, nearly empty, fallen over, or pigs might fly? Same question as the start, different coloured straws.

The team does

Shows how they feel about AI now.

The leader sees

Whether the room moved. Orange straws at the start, blue at the end, on the same board.
